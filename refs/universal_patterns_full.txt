1. The Compression of Reality into Internal World Models
At its core, any intelligent system receives high-dimensional, noisy sensory or tokenized data and compresses it into a continuous,
structured internal model.
Biological: The visual cortex takes millions of raw photon signals hitting the retina and compresses them into meaningful
abstractions (e.g., "edge," "motion," "predator").
Symbolic AI: Classical AI explicitly hard-coded these abstractions into ontologies, logic, and state graphs.
Deep Neural Networks: Networks map raw inputs into lower-dimensional latent spaces where geometric distances reflect
semantic relationships.
The universal goal is predictive abstraction: an intelligent system discards redundant spatial or temporal noise to keep only the
information required to predict what comes next.
2. Optimization Against Objective Functions (Free Energy / Loss Minimization)
Whether dictated by natural selection or gradient descent, intelligence operates by minimizing an error or surprise metric.
The Free Energy Principle (Neuroscience): Biological brains constantly act to minimize prediction errors—the gap between
what they expect to happen and what their senses report (often described mathematically as minimizing variational free
energy).
